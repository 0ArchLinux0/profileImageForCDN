## Card copy

### Card 1
**Reading feels familiar.**  
Can you remember it with the page closed?

*Design:* cream background, a notebook silhouette and a question card. Quiet blue/russet.

### Card 2
**1. Close your notes.**

Don't reread just yet.

*Design:* hand closing a notebook or own original illustration; no stock person's face needed.

### Card 3
**2. Answer from memory.**

Try three small questions.  
It's okay if you miss some.

*Design:* three empty prompt tiles; no fake performance chart.

### Card 4
**3. Open. Check. Correct.**

Compare your answer with the source.  
Mark the part you missed.

*Design:* one discrepancy highlight and a minimal correction annotation.

### Card 5
**Recall → Check → Revisit.**

A small self-test can be more useful than another passive read.

*Footer:* Roediger & Karpicke (2006); Dunlosky et al. (2013).

## Caption (copy ready)

A small learning method for an ordinary day:

One simple approach to try: close your notes, answer a few questions from memory, reopen the page, and correct the missing parts.

Research on retrieval practice suggests that actively recalling information can support long-term retention. The outcome depends on the material and how you use the method — it's a tool, not a guarantee.

Sources: Roediger & Karpicke (2006), https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x ; Dunlosky et al. (2013), https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
